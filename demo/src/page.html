Why fMRI and not EEG

Left: TRIBE v2's real predicted response to a stimulus at all 20 484 cortical points. Right: the same pattern as EEG would see it, blurred by the skull and sampled at a few scalp electrodes. Change the electrode count.

Say (50 s)Five reasons. Spatial detail: compare left and right. Only fMRI has a foundation encoder to invert. EEG adds a second ill-posed inverse, source localisation, before ours. Measured: the same decoder gets 32 percent character error from MEG and 67 from EEG. And there is no open EEG–fMRI set with sustained speech, which ruled out our fusion option in Review I. EEG wins on cost and speed, which matters for a product, not for measuring invertibility. The method transfers to EEG once an EEG encoder of this size exists. (The right panel is an illustration: neighbourhood smoothing plus electrode sampling, not a full head model.)
Method

Where we cut into the network

The public pipeline is not differentiable end to end. The input enters at one point only, after the feature extractors. We re-enter there, freeze every weight and optimise only the stimulus representation x.

Say (35 s)Speech synthesis, transcription and three big extractors are not differentiable. We found the single place the input batch is consumed, the aggregation into x, and rebuilt the forward map from there. It reproduces the released predictions exactly. Then x̂ = argmin of the squared error between f(x) and the target.
Asked at review · is the synthetic ground truth valid?

A known answer, checked against real stimuli

We choose x in TRIBE v2's own input space, push it through the frozen model, y = f(x), and recover x from y alone. Because we chose x, the error is exact. The question is whether that x looks like real stimuli.

Construction

8 random directions, cosine time courses

32 steps × 1 152 dimensions (text | audio | video slots, 16 s). Each of eight random unit directions carries its own cosine time course (2–16 cycles per window); the whole is scaled to unit s.d. Four seeds; white noise as an off-manifold control.

Correct by construction

Exact error, real operator

The standard known-answer test for inverse problems. The forward model is the released TRIBE v2 (re-entry r = 1.0000); nothing about the brain is assumed, so every recovery number is a measurement of the real inverse.

Shape does not matter

White noise · 64× speed · 4 seeds

White noise recovers like the structured truth (0.947 vs 0.942); a 64-fold change in speed of change leaves 0.836–0.858; results replicate across four seeds (agreement 0.894 ± 0.003).

Say (45 s)Asked at the review: how was the ground truth framed, and why is it right? It is a known-answer test: we pick x in the model's own input space, compute y = f(x) with the real frozen model, and recover x from y alone, so the error is exact. Is x realistic? Real story representations have about 5 effective dimensions, 84 percent of variance in 8 directions, the same scale; white noise and a 64-fold speed change do not change the result; and with a real 16-second stimulus as ground truth we see the same picture, stronger: agreement 0.19 to 0.56 and barriers of 11 to 21 times.

Five findings about the inverse

Say (2 min, one tab each)Dissociation: error down 28.4×, recovery saturates at 0.944. Non-convexity: fit-matched starts disagree, minimum cosine 0.817 over four ground truths. Barrier test with forward passes only: on the straight line between two distinct equally good blind fits the error rises 4.8× in the middle, and 8.1× towards a deeper minimum near the truth, over four ground truths, so these are separate minima, not a flat null space. Sensitivity: the solution difference is 2.15× less sensitive than the random median. Gain is identifiable; priors do not help; a ridge decoder reaches 0.06 against our 0.918.

Predictable is not recoverable

Pick a network. Its sensitivity is how strongly TRIBE v2 responds there; its recovery is how much of x comes back when only that network is observed, against random vertex sets of the same size.
