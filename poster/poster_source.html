Introduction

  • Encoding models predict fMRI from stimuli; decoding asks the reverse. Foundation encoders such as TRIBE v2 [1] now predict whole-cortex responses to video, audio and language.
  • If an encoder is accurate, can it simply be inverted to read out the stimulus representation? A good forward fit does not guarantee a well-posed inverse [2, 8].
  • We invert frozen TRIBE v2 by gradient descent on its 1 152-d multimodal representation and ask: is the inverse unique, how is it conditioned, and which cortex carries recoverable information?

Research Gap and Novelty

Earlier work inverts linear models, or uses deep encoders only in the forward direction. No prior study tests whether a deep foundation encoder's inverse is unique or how it is conditioned.

Result 1 · A good fit is not a good decode

Encoding error falls 28.4× across independent runs while recovery saturates near cosine 0.94; the final halving of error buys only +0.015.

Result 2 · Non-convex, yet identifiable

Initialisations at matched fit reach different solutions (pairwise cosine down to 0.835), reproducibly over 4 ground truths. Perturbations show barriers between minima, not a flat null space. The error lies along directions less sensitive than every random direction sampled (1.9× below median). Gain is identifiable: a 10% rescale gives 7.5× the noise floor.

Result 3 · Predictable is not recoverable

Visual cortex, the most responsive (1.60× cortex mean), recovers worst (0.63 vs 0.75 random). Somatomotor, the least responsive (0.73×), recovers best (0.85), above whole cortex. Control spread ≤ 0.013. Response diversity (participation ratio 18.6 vs 10.3) explains the extremes.

Conclusions

  • Inverting a foundation encoder is a non-convex, direction-dependently conditioned problem: forward accuracy does not certify decoding.
  • Recoverability must be measured, not inferred from predictivity.
  • On real fMRI the inverse identifies the heard moment but does not reconstruct its representation.

Real fMRI · first results

LeBel et al. [7], subject UTS03, held-out story; text + audio features. Encoding r = 0.48 on the top 2 000 vertices (56% of noise ceiling). Inverting measured BOLD picks the heard 100-s segment 4/5 times among 45 (chance 2.2%), yet cosine to the true x is only 0.02.
